First Principles

The Epistemological Charter

Why middle school mathematics must begin with real things.

In modern education, mathematics is too often reduced to arbitrary symbol-pushing on a screen or disconnected computational drills. Students are asked to manipulate signs before they have ever loved or understood the realities those signs represent.

The Curriculum of the River recovers the classical realist conviction articulated by St. Thomas Aquinas: Nihil est in intellectu quod non sit prius in sensu—nothing is in the intellect that was not first in the senses. The human soul is an embodied intellect; it cannot leap into pure abstraction without first contemplating an internal sensory image, the phantasm.

Every lesson in this curriculum is designed as an expansive single-sheet broadsheet folio, guiding the student through four distinct, unbroken movements of thought:

I. The Sensory Referent

A tactile, lived reality—water filling a reservoir, a balanced timber beam, or a wheel rolling across a bench. The eye and hand grasp the phenomenon first.

II. The Disciplinary Name

Giving the phenomenon its true, dignified mathematical name—Additive Inverse, Rate of Change, or Constant of Proportionality. No patronizing slang.

III. The Affixing Bridge

The operational proof that undoes the action—reversing addition by subtraction, unwrapping factors, proving that equilibrium holds true in both directions.

IV. The Abstract Invariant

The universal theorem ($y = mx + b$, $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$). Liberated from any single circumstance, the student beholds the unchanging order of the created world.

Grade 6 · Decad I

Fraction Division & The Geometry of Reciprocity

10-Day Decad Folio

Students often memorize "invert and multiply" without ever seeing why dividing by a fraction makes a quantity grow larger.

On Plate 1, fraction division begins as physical measurement: How many half-sized portions compose three whole units? Using continuous rectangular tape diagrams, students visually experience that each whole unit subdivides by two, doubling the count ($3 \times 2 = 6$).

Grade 7 · Decad I

Signed Numbers & Directional Equilibrium

10-Day Decad Folio

Negative numbers are not merely arithmetic subtractions—they are independent elements of the rational field possessing direction, magnitude, and opposition.

In Decad I, the rational numbers ($\mathbb{Q}$) are introduced through the concept of Zero Pairs and vector displacements. Physical elevation datums and opposite electrical charges demonstrate why $(-a)(-b) = +ab$: reversing an opposing direction restores positive alignment.

Grade 8 · Decad I

Linear Functions & The Affine Shift

10-Day Decad Folio

In Grade 7, direct proportions always passed through the origin: zero effort yielded zero result ($y = kx$).

In Grade 8, mathematics enters the physical world of non-zero baselines. On Plate 1, an initial fuel reserve shifts the proportional ray vertically by vector $\vec{T} = \langle 0, b \rangle$, introducing the affine function $y = mx + b$. Students observe how translation alters the simple ratio $y/x$ while leaving the rate of change ($\Delta y / \Delta x$) invariant.
